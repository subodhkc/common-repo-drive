Article 9 · Risk Management System

Scoped source-code findings and authorized runtime tests contribute risk-management evidence, including a finding owner and remediation backlog. These are technical inputs to the provider's risk management system.

Article 10 · Data and Data Governance

Data handling, minimization and relevant code-visible ingestion paths provide data-governance signals. Complete dataset provenance, representativeness and governance need the customer's wider records.

Article 11 · Technical Documentation

Inventory, system and version identity, evaluation receipts and evidence provenance supply traceable Annex IV documentation inputs. The provider maintains the full statutory technical document.

Article 12 · Record-Keeping and Logging

Code logging capability and connected event evidence can identify gaps in tool or inference traces, with source/observation limits. Actual operational logs and retention controls require verification.

Article 13 · Transparency and Provision of Information

Relevant AI-interaction disclosures, information patterns and documented use/limitation facts can be examined where visible in source. Complete provider instructions and user information are separate responsibilities.

Article 14 · Human Oversight

Agent action paths, human review checkpoints, delegation and policy evidence can support evaluation of technical oversight arrangements. Organizational human oversight remains an accountable operating process.

Article 15 · Accuracy, Robustness and Cybersecurity

AI AppSec, MCP tenant isolation, approved runtime testing and control findings provide scoped cybersecurity/robustness evidence, without replacing full accuracy validation or lifecycle requirements.

07 / Preserve the full obligation context

Which EU AI Act obligation family is relevant?

The law combines prohibitions, transparency, general-purpose model requirements, and category-specific high-risk duties. HAIEC connects these questions to relevant technical evidence where it can.

ARTICLE 5

Prohibited AI practices

Includes specified manipulative/exploitative AI practices, certain biometric applications and prohibited data-collection practices under detailed legal conditions and exceptions.

ARTICLE 50

Transparency

AI interaction disclosure and labeling of certain AI-generated or manipulated content, with obligations and timing depending on the application and actor.

GENERAL-PURPOSE MODELS

GPAI responsibilities

Documentation, policy and transparency-related duties for general-purpose AI model providers, plus extra duties for systemic-risk models.

ANNEX III

High-risk standalone use cases

Includes listed applications such as employment, education, essential services, biometric and public-sector categories, with applicability distinctions.

ANNEX I

Regulated-product AI

AI tied to certain product safety regimes follows the later product-linked high-risk application schedule.

MULTIPLE ROLES

Provider, deployer, importer and distributor

One organization may have more than one role. Legal classification and contractual facts determine which tasks belong to whom.

12 / Enterprise questions

Plan the next step with clarity.

Practical answers on readiness, EU AI Act scope, technical proof, software and consultation.

How is the HAIEC software assessment different from technical assurance?

The guided EU AI Act assessment collects structured system and role answers and produces scored gaps according to HAIEC's method. Technical assurance independently qualifies actual connected source, policy, permission, capability and observed evidence within a reviewed scope. Questionnaire scores are not official EU compliance percentages.

Can a US company need EU AI Act readiness?

Yes, EU-market and use-related reach can apply outside the EU depending on the company’s activity and the Act's terms. Confirm legal classification and relevant roles using the specific product and deployment facts.

Are all high-risk AI obligations due in August 2026?

No. Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 shifted the relevant Annex III Chapter III high-risk milestones to December 2, 2027 and Annex I product-linked dates to August 2, 2028, subject to transitional and other rules.

What is the difference between provider and deployer obligations?

Providers develop or place systems/models on the market and may carry technical documentation and conformity duties. Deployers use systems under their authority and have use-in-context responsibilities. Some organizations occupy multiple roles.

What can HAIEC establish for Article 14 human oversight?

In a scoped assessment it can evaluate agent action paths, source-visible review checkpoints, delegation and policy evidence and approved operational observations. Organizational oversight competence and deployment procedures remain separate facts to document.

Does a HAIEC evidence badge mean an AI system is certified?

No. A genuine issued HAIEC badge represents a particular artifact's status and proof scope. It is not a governmental EU AI Act certificate, CE mark or independent notified-body conformity judgment.

Can HAIEC monitor readiness as systems change?

Yes, for scoped approved integrations and evidence sources. Source, policy, permission, identity and runtime evidence can support reassessment and owner review. The observation and target-enforcement capabilities depend on the actual connection and deployment.

How do we start without providing production credentials?

Start with guided software assessment or a scoped consultation using existing technical documentation and approved source material. A more extensive technical review can qualify additional access and approved tests separately.
