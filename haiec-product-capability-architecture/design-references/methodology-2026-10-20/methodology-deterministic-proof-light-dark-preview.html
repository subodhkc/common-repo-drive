Authority and Delegated Action Integrity

Permission is not delegation.
Reachability is not execution.

Five independent evidence planes answer what was requested, policy-authorized, effectively granted, code-capable and observed. Separately, DAI examines whether consequential choice was actually delegated, within qualified scope and validity.

Requested

The task or caller intent as evidenced.

Policy authorized

The approved policy/operating boundary.

Effectively granted

Qualified credential/provider permissions.

Code capable

Implementation paths that could perform operations.

Observed

Only qualified recorded runtime behavior.

DAI: separate comparison

Authoritative delegated choice vs capable, granted and observed; absent delegation stays unresolved.

Research-to-implementation alignment gate: an approved Operating Envelope is policy evidence, not by itself proof of a human's delegation of consequential choice. This distinction must be verified in the DAI assembler before claiming full ISAF v2 semantic conformance.

Recorded output and boundaries

Four questions. One exact Evaluation.

Policy, grants and separately qualified delegation — no guessed approval.

What did it do?

Only actions actually witnessed in applicable runtime coverage.

Unmatched relations, failed producers, unavailable runtime or unsupported effects.

What can be verified?

Exact report/evidence membership and receipt integrity where issued; hash ≠ truth of the external world.

Does an approved policy mean an AI had delegated choice?

No. It can establish the policy boundary when qualified. Delegated authority needs an authoritative delegation record with exact protected choice and valid scope.

Does static reachability prove an operation occurred?

No. Code capability and observed action are separate evidence questions, and target-side business effect requires further proof.

Can a hash prove a runtime control blocked an action?

A hash can protect a recorded artifact's identity/integrity under its scheme. It cannot independently prove that a gateway enforced or downstream effect occurred.

Does “ALLOW” mean globally safe?

No. ALLOW means within the exact evaluated scope and qualifying evidence under the selected rules, not universal safety or certification.
