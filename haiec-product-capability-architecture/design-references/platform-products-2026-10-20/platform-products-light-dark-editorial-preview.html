THE PROBLEM

A tool permission does not tell you whether an action was authorized.

A scanner can show that code can call a refund API. IAM can show what a credential permits. Policy can define what needs approval. A log may show a call. None alone proves the complete delegation and consequence story.

Code-capableWhat the implementation can technically reach.
Effectively grantedWhat qualified credential/identity evidence makes available.
ObservedWhat authorized runtime evidence shows occurred within coverage.
Permission is not delegation. Capability is not execution. Missing evidence is not a pass.

EVIDENCE-BOUND ASSURANCE

One consequential action. Five evidence questions.

What was requested, authorized, effectively granted, code-capable and observed?

HAIEC compares these independently qualified dimensions against a bounded question. A detected action path is not automatically delegated, executed or proven to have changed a target.

Explore the methodology →

What you can review

  • Action and access map within supported coverage
  • Deterministic evaluation and missing evidence
  • Human decision records where configured
  • Scoped reports and verification artifacts

Illustrative example: A support agent can call a refund endpoint, but the evidence does not establish delegated authority to choose the recipient. That is a review question, not a universal safe verdict.
