Frequently asked questions

Where DAI fits: and what it changes.

What is Delegated Action Integrity?

DAI is HAIEC's bounded method of comparing supported delegated decision authority with code capability, effective access and relevant observations across nine dimensions. It helps identify potential consequential overreach rather than judging the AI's prose.

Isn't IAM permission enough?

IAM can establish whether a principal has technical access. It doesn't always establish the business authority to choose a customer, beneficiary, destination or other consequential outcome.

Do I need to upload delegation documents before HAIEC can find risks?

No. Authorized source/code analysis can surface consequential paths and candidate exposure without a completed delegation history or runtime connection. Additional authority evidence matters to stronger, specific business-decision claims.

What if an approved policy already determines the choice?

An explicit policy plus trusted applicable facts may fully determine the choice. The system still needs to compare the actual selected value before claiming compliance. The outcome depends on the selected value and the rule's applicable evidence.

Does potential reachability prove an unauthorized transaction occurred?

No. A source-backed capability finding is distinct from a runtime observation and a confirmed target-side effect. You can investigate a supported risk before such an event is observed.

How does DAI relate to the five evidence planes?

Requested, Policy Authorized, Effectively Granted, Code Capable and Observed are separate evidence questions. DAI is a cross-cutting delegated-choice comparison: not a sixth plane or mandatory sixth checkpoint.

Is DAI a seventh paid feature or capability?

No. It is part of HAIEC's assurance approach. Access to particular reports or evaluations depends on actual plan, organization permissions, source eligibility and supported production functionality.

Does HAIEC automatically block agents when DAI detects a problem?

No universal blocking is implied. Installed target-side protection requires an authorized, integrated execution point and separate verification. DAI findings can inform prevention or human decisions.

Can I run HAIEC through an AI IDE or MCP client?

Yes, where the client supports compatible MCP and the customer has configured scoped credentials and authorized sources. HAIEC remains the official evaluator; the AI client guides authorized tool calls.

What makes HAIEC different from using another LLM to evaluate an agent?

HAIEC's canonical rules operate over supported source and evidence facts and yield bounded, versioned results. An AI assistant may explain or operate the workflow but cannot replace the canonical evaluator's recorded result.
