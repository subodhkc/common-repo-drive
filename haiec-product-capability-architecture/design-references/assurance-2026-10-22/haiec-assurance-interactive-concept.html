THE HAIEC ASSURANCE MODEL

Five independent views. No guessed connections.

A security scan can establish a code-capable route. IAM can establish a qualified grant. Policy can establish a permitted boundary. Runtime evidence can establish an observed action. None silently proves another.

DAI compares the consequential choice.

It tests the relationship between available delegation facts, approved scope and actual capability or activity. It is a comparator, not a sixth evidence plane.

The deterministic evaluator produces a bounded answer.

Qualified evidence, applicable rules and proof limits determine the recorded conclusion. AI can operate the workflow, but it does not author the canonical verdict.

QUESTIONS BUYERS ASK

Clear answers.
Defined limits.

Assurance is strongest when every conclusion says exactly which evidence supports it.

Is assurance just security plus governance?

Security and governance contribute important questions and evidence. Assurance evaluates relevant facts, authority, actual capability and observation to reach a scoped conclusion. It is not a simple sum or an additional scanner.

Can a security scan establish delegation?

Not by itself. A scan can establish code-capable behavior within its scope. Delegated discretion requires appropriate policy or human authority evidence.

Does unknown evidence mean the system failed?

No. Unknown means the available evidence cannot establish the claim. It is different from a verified violation and different from a passed check.

Does a HAIEC BLOCK outcome automatically stop the action?

No. The outcome records an evaluator disposition. Target-side enforcement requires a separately connected and supported control with evidence of its actual operation.

Can I start without connecting every data source?

Yes. Supported early findings, such as a source-visible consequential path, can be valuable. The depth of the subsequent assurance conclusion depends on the evidence needed for the chosen claim.
