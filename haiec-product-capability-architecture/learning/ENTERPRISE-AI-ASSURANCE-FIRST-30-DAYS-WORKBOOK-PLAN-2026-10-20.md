# Enterprise AI Assurance: Your First 30 Days | HAIEC Workbook Plan

**Date:** 2026-10-20. **State:** planning skeleton only. No HTML created, platform changed or customer evaluation run.

## Purpose

Customer-friendly, self-learning 30-day workbook that translates the existing HAIEC Crash Course into real actions, accountable decisions and repeatable assurance operations. One consequential AI system, one principal assurance question and minimum eligible evidence source. Do not require all integrations or a complete inventory before showing a valid finding.

Three pillars: Understand, Assure, Govern & Operate.
Six independently selectable capabilities: Discover, Evaluate, Authorize, Monitor, Protect, Verify.
Five independent evidence planes: Requested, Policy Authorized, Effectively Granted, Code Capable, Observed.

A 30-day *learning and adoption plan* is not a promise of a live production pilot or full enterprise POC in 30 days. Trial duration, capabilities and consent are governed by the actual account and agreement, not the workbook.

## Who uses it

**Executive sponsor** owns value, scope and acceptance.
**AI system owner** owns agent/business action and system context.
**Security/assurance reviewer** owns factual findings and control questions.
**Evidence/IAM owner** supplies only needed qualified source information.
**Governance reviewer** owns authorized human decisions.
**Operations owner** owns monitoring, incident response and renewal.
Roles can overlap where legitimate, but review approval must satisfy actual authorization and separation-of-duties constraints.

**Explore mode:** Public Kestrel example, educational worksheets, no HAIEC account requirement.
**Workspace mode:** Authorized company system, eligible source and evaluation access, exact actual evaluation/report IDs when executed.
Either mode may begin from the System, the Goal or an Evidence Source.

## Four-week learning roadmap

| Period | Question | Outcome and check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Days 1–7: Discover | What can the selected AI do and what matters? | One scoped consequential action, owner, qualified source status, first technical finding or exact blocker |
| Days 8–14: Evaluate | What do source, policy, permission, DAI and controls establish? | Bounded evaluation where entitled, evidence-plane matrix, applicable control checks, limitations |
| Days 15–21: Govern | Who decides and who will respond? | Responsible human review, approved/absent policy clearly labeled, remediation with owners, protection feasibility |
| Days 22–30: Operate | How do we know when something changed, and show proof? | Source health and response plan, verification links where issued, executive readout and 30/60/90 operating plan |

**Flexible:** The sequence teaches an illustrative customer journey. HAIEC's capabilities are independent; do not gate source findings until day 7 or require all six jobs. User permission or actual source state can legitimately block actions. Teaching completion does not create HAIEC approval or a valid report.

## Day-30 executive scorecard

- Original consequential action and business question, in-scope environment and owner.
- Known at start vs established now vs still unassessed, with exact provenance.
- Count of qualified action paths with a correct denominator.
- Number of actual applicable controls tested, not a claim all 45 are operating.
- Any verified policy, effective grant, code capability, observation and DAI mismatch, kept separate.
- Real human actions and assigned remediation, not automated approval.
- Monitoring source freshness, actual alert acknowledgement and target enforcement only where independently proven.
- Actual evaluation, report and receipt references and visibility status, where issued.
- Named triggers to re-evaluate and 30/60/90 follow-up.

## Learning modes and worksheets

The eventual HTML should offer **Explore the public example** and **Work in my authorized HAIEC workspace**. Every card shows: why it matters, what to do, required access, example/source link, what can be established, what remains unknown, and a small editable non-sensitive output.

Light-theme, one standalone HTML file with four weekly tabs, 30 expandable cards, progress, glossary, optional role filter, five-plane diagram, DAI educational choice exercise, control status legend, monitoring/decision diagram, reference links and printable report. Accessible mobile sources, keyboard support, offline read mode and readable PDF print. No user-facing code that pretends to invoke HAIEC engines.

Optional progressive templates: objective/scope; AI system/consequence; source register; five-plane matrix; DAI dimensions; applicable controls; exact evaluation; human decision; monitoring/response; report/receipt index; 30-day executive scorecard and next 30/60/90 plan. Do not make these mandatory upload forms.

## Source and product truth requirements

- Finding-first, no all-source prerequisite. Code capability is not executed effect.
- Policy-approved evidence remains valid even when protected-choice delegation is unresolved. Current DAI v1 and proposed ISAF v2 research are not identical.
- Exactly 45 current control catalog entries including specialized C7/C9/C16. Catalog, implementation, execution and target enforcement differ.
- Evaluate disposition is bounded ALLOW/REVIEW/BLOCK, not human deployment approval.
- Monitoring configured is not fresh source; alert delivered is not human acknowledgment.
- Customer-native action guard is not automatically wired; runtime protection requires qualified target integration.
- Evidence ingested is not evidence evaluated; a signed receipt does not certify upstream facts.
- MCP uses scoped API-key Bearer and user-approved operations; no customer secrets collected in learning form.
- Preserve separation of product-native core vs optional tailored enterprise integration. Never use lesson completion as billing or a tier claim.
