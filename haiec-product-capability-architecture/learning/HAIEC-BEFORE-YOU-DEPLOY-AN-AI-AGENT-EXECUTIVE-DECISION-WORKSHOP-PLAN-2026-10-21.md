## 3. Executive decision model: four layers, no invented automation

1. **ASSURANCE RESULT:** What specific HAIEC evidence qualified what finding, disposition and frontier? Report ALLOW/REVIEW/BLOCK are evidence-limited.
2. **HUMAN OPERATING DECISION:** Who with appropriate authority may approve only specified operations, conditions, environment and timeframe, and what is expressly prohibited? Stored states and currentness matter.
3. **ACTUAL RUNTIME/NATIVE PROTECTION:** What customer-native tool, policy enforcement point and independently sourced target outcome exist? Configured guard, accepted dispatch and verified target effect are not equivalent.
4. **RESIDUAL RISK ACCOUNTABILITY:** What exposure remains, what controls/treatment apply, who accepts it if permitted under policy, what evidence must arrive, and what triggers pause, rollback, re-evaluation or expiration?

**Risk treatment in plain language:** Avoid or defer the operation; restrict it; implement and prove necessary controls; escalate to accountable reviewers; accept only specifically permitted residual risk with owner/time/scope; revisit after change. "Transfer to vendor" does not remove enterprise responsibility. Missing evidence is never automatically a pass, failure or prohibition; severity, consequence and risk tolerance determine the next requirement.

**Do not introduce a numeric "ready to deploy" score.** The app can show progress through teaching and a selected proposed next action, but cannot generate a real HAIEC U5/U6 verdict, risk opinion or binding human approval.

## 4. Three high-value scenarios and twelve author-authored decisions

**Canonical education fixture:** [Three-case / 12-decision blueprint](HAIEC-EXECUTIVE-AI-AGENT-DEPLOYMENT-DECISION-WORKSHOP-3-CASE-12-DECISION-BLUEPRINT-2026-10-21.json), with 3 option choices, correct answer, detailed explanation, distractor reasons, exact five-plane facts, source questions, risk owners and proposed bounded next action for every case.

### Case 1: The AI can change supplier bank details

- **Initial facts:** Demonstration works, repository scan establishes code path to bank-detail mutation; policy, principal IAM, real target intervention and actual action effects unestablished.
- **Business exposure:** Misrouted payments, supplier fraud, treasury loss.
- **Four executive decisions:** Correct source interpretation; minimum relevant evidence; safe scoping for a pilot; explicit risk owner/conditions.
- **Supported response:** A strictly limited pilot for separately approved low-impact tasks may be considered. Bank-detail mutation remains excluded pending relevant authority, evidence and owner approval. Do not call the whole AI globally blocked or automatically safe.

### Case 2: The vendor AI wants CRM/customer-data access

- **Initial facts:** Vendor SOC 2 and model test claims supplied, but the enterprise has not qualified exact integration tool scopes, tenant/object authorization, data egress or runtime witnesses.
- **Business exposure:** Customer privacy, cross-tenant reads, improper write/export access.
- **Four executive decisions:** Interpret the vendor evidence; require relevant integration-bound proof; allow synthetic least-privilege evaluation vs production data; assign enterprise data/risk owners.
- **Supported response:** Isolated synthetic-data trial can be considered. Vendor source inaccessible stays unresolved rather than falsely certified clean.

### Case 3: Yesterday's approval is stale after a release

- **Initial facts:** Older bounded ALLOW and human approval; new source/policy version and additional consequential operation; provider accepted an attempt, independent target result not shown.
- **Business exposure:** Network/industrial or operational state changes beyond old authorized conditions.
- **Four executive decisions:** Old approval currentness; acceptance versus effect; re-approval requirements for new operation; residual risk and monitoring/expiry conditions.
- **Supported response:** No automatic expansion. Perform fresh source/authority/guard checks and a new accountable scoped decision. Unchanged approved operations need separate context and can continue only when their own requirements remain satisfied.

### Six interactive counterfactuals

- A new **policy approval** appears: policy claim may change, but live IAM and observed target effects do not.
- **Effective principal grant** becomes qualified: authorized credential operation does not prove discretionary business delegation.
- **Native target block** is independently witnessed for one exact action: narrow protection claim strengthens, not universal protection.
- A **revised commit or deployment** replaces the evaluated version: historic finding remains, current reliance/approval must be checked.
- An **approval expires or is revoked**: historic decision still documented but not current operating authority.
- **Monitoring receives a log** after the snapshot: ongoing evidence can be investigated, but does not silently change the frozen evaluation.

Each interactive change must update only the pedagogically justified claims, with a clear "what changed, what did not" explanation.

## 5. Executive course experience: seven succinct sections

**Total target:** 18 to 22 minutes self-guided; sales demo 8 to 10; optional facilitator 30 to 45.

1. **Hero: Your AI pilot works. Is your business ready to let it act?** A strong consequence map with buyer problem examples and one click to begin. No long glossary.
2. **The Four Decisions Executives Must Separate.** Assurance, accountable approval, native protection, residual risk. Visual business-action flow and 90-second introduction.
3. **Three Decision Rooms.** Choose Finance, Vendor Data or Changed Release. Each has a business context, structured evidence cards, four choice gates, immediate bounded explanations and an executive recommendation. User can skip and return without losing current selections.
4. **The Risk Treatment Board.** Four treatments: proceed in a narrow approved envelope, request more evidence, remediate/retest, do not expand/stop. These are suggested commercial decision pathways, not HAIEC U5 statuses. Show mitigation owners and triggers.
5. **Read the Proof, Not the Badge.** A compact source-qualified Kestrel example and report excerpt/explanation, real link, 44 paths and REVIEW with explicit gaps. Separate real report from invented scenarios and never invent production actions/receipts.
6. **Your Executive Decision Memo.** Editor for named business question, system/operation, verified source facts, permission/policy/currentness, treatment, allowed/prohibited operations, accountable decision maker, risk owner, conditions, evidence next, expiry/review and trigger for stopping. Download/print locally only.
7. **Next Steps for Your Team.** Executive checklist, optional deep links to Integration Field Guide, DAI Lab, Report Trust Plan and Enterprise POC. Canonical Enterprise conversion CTA, no second contact form.

**Content density target:** Brief narrative cards, no enormous 45-control table, no 76-activity enumeration, no internal code words in headings. A sidebar/appendix provides definitions on demand.

## 6. Five diagrams with purposeful meaning

1. **Consequence Chain:** AI agent → business tool → authorized server boundary → sensitive customer operation → possible economic/operational consequence. Show "reachable" separately from "performed".
2. **Decision Separation:** Assurance finding, human operating decision, native target intervention and residual-risk ownership as four equal visual lanes. A bright state in one must not color the rest green.
3. **Five Evidence Lens:** Requested, Policy Authorized, Effectively Granted, Code Capable, Observed as independent peers with unknown/unassessed; target witness as distinct additional proof. Hide technical labels behind reader-friendly plain terms.
4. **Four Risk Treatments:** A branching, non-automated executive choice map with case-specific triggers and required owners. Never treat a preferred choice as a real approval.
5. **Time/Change/Expiry:** Evaluation snapshot, policy approval, human decision, release change and new target witness on a timeline. Highlight stale policy/evaluation/expired approval separately.

CSS/SVG/DOM illustrations accessible with native text descriptions, subtle purposeful animation, reduced motion, keyboard operation, portable print render and no decorative dashboard theater or false "live approved" badge.

## 7. Board-ready memo output design

**Memo title:** Executive AI Deployment Decision Brief | Educational Workshop Draft
**Identity:** AI system name (user input only), enterprise environment, evaluation/source reference if genuinely supplied, operation/target, executive decision question.
**Facts:** Established code capability, applicable policy, effective permission, observed action and additional target consequence evidence, each with source or explicitly NOT_PROVIDED.
**Risk:** Main business impact, possible affected users/data/processes, risk treatment, existing controls and limitations, highest consequence not silently excluded.
**Decision:** Draft recommendation lane (proceed to appropriately scoped pilot / remediate and retest / evidence upgrade / stop or no expansion). This is an **educational field** and not a persisted HAIEC status.
**Authority:** Named decision sponsor, separate security/risk reviewer, prohibited vs allowed actions, requirement for human signoff in customer's actual process; reviewer self-approval invalid where separation of duties applies.
**Operating conditions:** Scope/expiry, external target or guard ownership, monitoring/coverage requirement when relevant, escalation/stop conditions, fresh review triggers for changed code/policy/IAM/tools.
**Evidence request:** Named exact source and owner, request priority, earliest adequate next check, unassessed facts and effect limitations.
**Publication note:** Exporting or printing this draft does not create a HAIEC decision receipt, approval, evaluation, target control or regulatory certification.

Provide local-only Markdown and JSON export plus print-to-PDF via browser. No user information sent to HAIEC from the standalone artifact. Do not store sensitive text in localStorage by default. If an export references a public example, label it as a *reference*, never as customer evidence.
