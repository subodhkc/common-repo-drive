The problem02 · Fragmented security
The problem

Your security tools each answer critical questions. HAIEC helps reconcile what an AI is authorized to do with what it can and does do.

What your stack answers — separately

IAM & Access
Who can authenticate. Not: is this business action authorized?
AppSec & CI
What code can reach. Not: is the reachable capability approved for this system?
SIEM & Monitoring
What events occurred. Not: was what occurred permitted to occur?
GRC & Framework
What policy exists. Not: did the AI actually operate inside it?
Model Guardrails & Firewalls
What the model says. Not: what tools it invokes or what target gets affected.
The authority gap
An AI agent can be correctly authenticated and still act outside its intended business responsibility.
A refund agent may be instructed not to issue large refunds, while its API credential permits much larger transactions. A quality agent may be authorized to update one work order, while its credential can update any work order in the plant. The controls work as configured. The authority-to-action is not intact.
HAIEC's premise: the difference between access permission, technical capability, delegated authority, and actual behavior is where consequential AI risk lives.
You already have IAM, AppSec, SIEM, GRC. HAIEC doesn't replace them. It reconciles their evidence around the AI system and its authorized actions — the question none of them was designed to answer alone.

Assurance & authority

Five independent evidence planes. DAI as a separate authority comparison. Deterministic result at every step.

01
Requested
What the AI system asks to do.
02
Policy-authorized
What the business permits.
03
Effectively granted
What credentials allow.
04
Code-capable
What code can reach.
05
Observed
What runtime evidence shows.

Comparison, not sequence

The five planes are independent assertions. HAIEC does not require them to be supplied in order, and does not require all five to reach a finding. A single-plane evaluation is valid — it names fewer established facts.

The mismatch is the finding. Requested but not policy-authorized → policy gap. Policy-authorized but not effectively granted → provisioning gap. Effectively granted but not code-reachable → dead privilege. Code-reachable but not observed → latent exposure.

DevSecOps07 · CI/CD & release
DevSecOps & release governance

Four separate facts about a release. Never collapsed into one gate status.

Code diff
PR opened → source analyzed
GitHub App static analysis on changed files. Full-tree coverage requires full scan.
Static evidence
AI AppSec · secrets · dependencies
Findings at scan scope. Package attribution preserved. Not automatically qualified.
Assurance
Deterministic engine · scoped disposition
ALLOW / REVIEW / BLOCK over the qualified evaluation scope. Not a deployment decision.
Human approval
Named reviewer · digest-bound record
Separation of duties. Approval is a decision about the scope. Not execution.
CI gate
Independently configured pass/fail
CI gate is a pipeline policy. Not the engine disposition. Not the human approval.
Ready
GitHub App full executor on linked installations. Full static + PR comments + qualified evidence.
Held
GitHub Action v2 pending reconciliation. v1 PAT flow is deprecated. Do not build new workflows against v1.
Preview
Local/CI bundle path. Bounded coverage by bundle contents. Full static requires complete source tree.
CI_RESULT ≠ ASSURANCE_ENGINE_DISPOSITION ≠ HUMAN_APPROVAL ≠ DEPLOYMENT_BLOCK. A CI gate passing does not mean HAIEC evaluated or approved the broader system. A REVIEW disposition does not automatically block a pipeline. Building a release process on the wrong fact produces the wrong outcome at exactly the wrong time.

The POC12 · Enterprise POC
Scoped enterprise POC

Evaluate one consequential AI system. Prove the value before expanding.

01

What you provide

  • One consequential AI system (non-production preferred where practical) with a named business owner
  • Read access to its source repository, or a source archive
  • Its operating envelope or the intent it should be governed by
  • Its IAM grants — via live observer or signed JSON export
  • Optional: 7–14 days OTLP traces or gateway events
02

What HAIEC does

  • Maps the system's consequential capabilities and authority planes
  • Runs deterministic assurance over supplied evidence
  • Compares authority-to-action across five planes + DAI
  • Produces a scoped disposition and a frozen receipt
  • Surfaces the proof frontier — what remains unknown
03

What you get

  • Executive assurance summary (findings, disposition, scope)
  • Technical evidence appendix with per-analyzer participation
  • Digest-bound receipt for auditor or customer review
  • Prioritized remediation list with named unknowns
  • Architectural recommendation for scaling to the next 3 systems

Success criteria — clean, inconclusive, or finding all valid outcomes

Success 01
Qualified evidence basis
Every source authenticated, source-bound, and schema-qualified against a registered binder.
Success 02
Deterministic rerun stability
Same input produces the same findings and digest. Changes between snapshots are visible.
Success 03
Traceable finding OR clean result
A qualified finding, a clean result, or a declared inconclusive outcome — each defensible. Vulnerability discovery is not a requirement.
Success 04
Defensible decision artifact
An evidence-bound report or receipt that your security, legal or audit team can review within its stated scope.

Typically 4–8 weeks after readiness · One system · One defensible answer to: was this AI authorized to do what it did?
